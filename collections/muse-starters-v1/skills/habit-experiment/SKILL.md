# Try a Small Habit Change and Review What Happens

## Use when
A person wants a manageable routine experiment rather than an all-or-nothing plan.

## Not for
Diagnosing conditions, changing treatment, impersonating a professional, or assuming access to another person’s private records.

## Inputs
- The desired change and current routine
- Time, energy, environmental constraints and preferred tracking

## Procedure
1. Choose one small behavior and a practical cue.
2. Identify a likely obstacle and a lower-effort fallback.
3. Agree on a short observation period without manufacturing a recurring notification.
4. Track only the observations the person wants to provide.
5. Review what helped or interfered and adjust without treating a missed day as failure.

## Deliver
- A small routine experiment
- A nonjudgmental review prompt

## Check the result
- The experiment fits the person’s constraints.
- Tracking is optional and proportionate.
- Health or performance benefits are not guaranteed.

## Recover or narrow
Keep a useful organizational or writing result without presenting it as medical, legal or therapeutic authority. For urgent safety concerns, direct the user to appropriate real-world help.

## Example request
Help me try a ten-minute evening tidy for a week with a fallback for exhausted days.

## Design refinement
Adds an explicit fallback and learning-oriented review instead of rigid streak pressure.
