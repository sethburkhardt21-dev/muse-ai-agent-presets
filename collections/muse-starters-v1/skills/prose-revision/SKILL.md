# Revise Prose Without Losing Voice or Meaning

## Use when
A draft needs clearer structure, tone or rhythm while preserving its substance.

## Not for
Claiming a plan is rendered media, ignoring reference constraints, or publishing assets without appropriate rights.

## Inputs
- The draft and intended reader
- The requested voice and protected wording

## Procedure
1. Read the entire source and determine whether the request authorizes editing or only critique.
2. Mark exact protected spans, factual claims, uncertainty qualifiers, audience and length constraints.
3. Choose the smallest structural and sentence-level edits that achieve the requested purpose while retaining the author's register.
4. For voice adaptation, use supplied samples or explicit preferences rather than invented personality; routine cleanup does not require calibration.
5. Compare original and revised claims, numbers, names and protected spans; repair any semantic strengthening or omitted condition.
6. Deliver the finished text and a concise material-change note, leaving unsupported claims unresolved rather than embellishing them.

## Deliver
- A complete revised draft
- A brief account of material meaning changes when needed
- Return the complete requested revision or audit, not advice in place of text. Keep protected spans exact, preserve factual scope and audience, and explain only consequential changes. A style detector is advisory; authorship or truth cannot be inferred from a phrase score.

## Check the result
- No new factual claims are introduced without support.
- The requested voice survives.
- The revision is finished prose, not merely advice about prose.
- The task-specific structured output is checked against source facts or disclosed adjudication, while semantic usefulness still requires human review.

## Recover or narrow
Save the source version, edit mode, protected-span list, factual qualifiers and approved output. Resume from the unresolved editorial issue, not a new voice-calibration interview or an overwrite of the original.
