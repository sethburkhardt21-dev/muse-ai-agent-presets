# Adapt a Routine to the Person’s Stated Needs

## Use when
A person wants an everyday routine made easier to read, follow or carry out.

## Not for
Diagnosing conditions, changing treatment, impersonating a professional, or assuming access to another person’s private records.

## Inputs
- The actual task and self-described access needs
- Preferred communication format and available supports

## Procedure
1. Ask about functional needs without inferring a diagnosis.
2. Break the task into meaningful steps and identify the most difficult transition.
3. Offer a format suited to the person, such as plain language, larger text or one step at a time.
4. Remove unnecessary decisions and add a simple recovery option for interruption.
5. Let the person correct the routine and preserve their autonomy.

## Deliver
- An accessible task sequence
- A low-friction interruption recovery option

## Check the result
- Adaptations follow stated needs rather than stereotypes.
- The person retains control of decisions.
- Simpler presentation does not omit important safety information.

## Recover or narrow
Keep a useful organizational or writing result without presenting it as medical, legal or therapeutic authority. For urgent safety concerns, direct the user to appropriate real-world help.

## Example request
Make this morning checklist easier to follow with one step at a time and a way to resume after interruptions.

## Design refinement
Adds individualized format and interruption support without assuming disability from limited information.
