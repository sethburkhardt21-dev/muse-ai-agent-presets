# Shape an Offer Around a Specific Outcome — operational depth

Read only the branch needed for the current task. These are working methods, not a demand for additional approvals on ordinary reading or drafting.

## Domain method

Shape an offer around a specific customer outcome, a deliverable, a service boundary and evidence the provider can actually supply. Separate the promise from a guarantee. Define what the customer contributes, what is excluded, how completion is accepted and what happens when assumptions fail.

Model capacity and contribution economics explicitly. Revenue is not margin: variable delivery cost, fixed setup cost and the number of customers change the scenario. Cash timing and refundable deposits are separate from economic cost. A profitable spreadsheet is only a scenario until demand, execution cost and acceptance are observed.

## Output contract

Deliver an offer brief with audience, outcome, deliverables, exclusions, customer prerequisites, acceptance, delivery capacity and transparent price/cost scenarios. Identify unsupported claims and a bounded pilot. Do not invent testimonials, guaranteed results or a payment commitment.

## Choose the right action

### The user has skills but no concrete offer.
Choose a narrow outcome that can be delivered and verified with existing capabilities.

**Avoid:** Do not inflate the promise to cover every possible customer need.

### The scope expands during the discussion.
Separate the core deliverable from optional additions and expose their time and cost.

**Avoid:** Do not hide extra work behind an unchanged price.

### The price exceeds variable cost but fixed costs are material.
Calculate per-customer contribution and the full scenario after fixed cost.

**Avoid:** Do not report revenue or gross receipts as profit.

### Demand would exceed delivery capacity.
Reduce the pilot volume, change sequencing or identify a real resource gap.

**Avoid:** Do not promise dates that assume nonexistent staff or tools.

### The offer depends on an unsupported result claim.
Replace the claim with an honest deliverable or an explicitly tested outcome.

**Avoid:** Do not manufacture case studies or imply an unmeasured guarantee.

## Worked example (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** Price is 200 dollars, variable cost is 120, fixed setup is 100, and the pilot has three customers.

**Method:** Calculate contribution before comparing the pilot with available capacity.

**Result:** Contribution is 80 dollars per customer; scenario margin is 140 dollars after fixed setup. Pilot suitability still depends on supported scope and actual capacity.

## Edge or recovery example 1 (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** Five customers request the same pilot but capacity is four.

**Method:** Preserve the economics but evaluate the capacity constraint separately.

**Result:** The scenario may have positive margin, yet the five-customer offer is not delivery-ready. Cap or sequence intake rather than pretending capacity is unlimited.

## Failure diagnosis

### The offer is attractive but impossible to deliver consistently.
**Likely cause:** Acceptance and customer prerequisites were vague.

**Repair:** Write an observable done condition and required customer inputs.

**Recheck:** Both parties can distinguish complete delivery from an open-ended promise.

### A high price is mistaken for a healthy margin.
**Likely cause:** Delivery cost and fixed setup were omitted.

**Repair:** Recalculate contribution and scenario margin with stated assumptions.

**Recheck:** The arithmetic reconciles to price, unit cost and volume.

### A pilot generates more work than the team can handle.
**Likely cause:** Capacity was not part of the offer.

**Repair:** Cap the pilot and define intake or waiting-list behavior.

**Recheck:** Committed work remains within actual capacity.

## Preserve for the next turn

Preserve the offer version, supported claims, deliverables and exclusions, acceptance criteria, cost assumptions, capacity and actual pilot observations. Resume from changed constraints; do not rewrite a tentative offer as a signed agreement.
