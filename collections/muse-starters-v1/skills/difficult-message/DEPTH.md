# Write a Difficult Message With a Clear Ask — operational depth

Read only the branch needed for the current task. These are working methods, not a demand for additional approvals on ordinary reading or drafting.

## Domain method

Build a difficult message from observable facts, the sender’s own experience, a concrete request and a proportionate boundary. Separate an interpretation of someone’s intent from what actually happened. Adapt tone to the relationship, channel and consequence without erasing the sender’s meaning.

Use enough specificity for the recipient to respond, but avoid unnecessary accusations, diagnoses or threats. A boundary describes what the sender will do or accept; it is not a fabricated authority over the recipient. High-stakes employment, legal or safety situations may need qualified advice beyond drafting. Sending remains a separate authorized action.

## Output contract

Deliver a ready-to-review draft, its concrete ask and any unresolved facts or commitments. Preserve the user’s intended tone and true circumstances. Provide alternatives only when they change a meaningful tradeoff, not a long menu of cosmetic rewrites.

## Choose the right action

### The facts and requested outcome are clear.
Write the draft directly and keep the ask visible.

**Avoid:** Do not restart an emotional interview when the user has supplied enough context.

### The draft attributes motives without evidence.
Reframe as an observed event or clearly labeled interpretation.

**Avoid:** Do not diagnose the recipient or assert hidden intent as fact.

### A boundary is needed.
State the sender’s action, condition and realistic follow-through.

**Avoid:** Do not add a threat the sender cannot or does not intend to carry out.

### The message could disclose private information.
Remove unrelated details and check the intended audience.

**Avoid:** Do not include another person’s sensitive history merely to strengthen an argument.

### The user asks to send the message.
Confirm the exact recipient and final authorized text through the actual available provider.

**Avoid:** Do not confuse a draft with a sent or received message.

## Worked example (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** A missed deadline is verified, but the sender’s belief that it was deliberate is unconfirmed.

**Method:** Use the missed deadline as fact and avoid asserting intent.

**Result:** Draft: the deadline passed, this affected the work, and a revised delivery date is requested. Deliberate disregard is not stated as proven.

## Edge or recovery example 1 (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** The user explicitly prefers a firmer boundary after repeated verified misses.

**Method:** Preserve the documented pattern and state the sender’s realistic next action.

**Result:** A firmer draft can set a clear condition without fabricated threats; sending remains unperformed.

## Failure diagnosis

### The recipient cannot tell what response is requested.
**Likely cause:** The message contains feelings but no concrete ask.

**Repair:** Add one specific action or question with a realistic timeframe when appropriate.

**Recheck:** A reasonable recipient can identify the next response.

### The draft escalates the conflict unnecessarily.
**Likely cause:** Interpretations were presented as accusations.

**Repair:** Separate facts from inferences and retain a proportionate boundary.

**Recheck:** The draft remains firm without inventing motives.

### A rewrite no longer sounds like the user.
**Likely cause:** Tone optimization replaced the user’s intent.

**Repair:** Restore their level of directness and protected wording.

**Recheck:** The final draft expresses the user’s actual position.

## Preserve for the next turn

Preserve verified facts, interpretations, protected wording, relationship and channel, the concrete ask, commitments and actual sending state. Resume from the approved draft rather than generating a new emotional narrative.
