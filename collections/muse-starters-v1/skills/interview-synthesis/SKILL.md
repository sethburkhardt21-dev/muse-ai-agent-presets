# Synthesize Interviews Without Inventing Consensus

## Use when
Interview notes need to become findings while retaining dissent and provenance.

## Not for
Treating a popularity signal, search snippet or confident summary as sufficient evidence for a factual conclusion.

## Inputs
- Authorized notes or transcripts
- The research question and participant labels

## Procedure
1. Inventory the supplied transcripts, consent boundaries and participant IDs; mark missing sessions before synthesis.
2. Read each interview in context and record excerpts with stable locators rather than isolated memorable sentences.
3. Develop a concise codebook with inclusion and exclusion notes; revise it transparently when a new distinction matters.
4. Group related codes into themes while keeping each supporting and dissenting participant visible.
5. Calculate only within-corpus counts using distinct participants and a stated denominator; retain nonresponse and not-asked separately.
6. Translate the strongest findings into bounded recommendations and unresolved questions, without claiming a tested product improvement.

## Deliver
- A traceable theme map
- Findings with dissent and next questions
- Return a theme table with participant IDs and exact excerpt references, observed behavior versus interpretation, divergent evidence, sampling limits and a next research or product decision. Remove identifying details not necessary to the finding. Do not invent demographic personas or effect sizes.

## Check the result
- Quotes retain their original meaning.
- One vocal participant does not become a majority.
- Sample limitations remain visible.
- The task-specific structured output is checked against source facts or disclosed adjudication, while semantic usefulness still requires human review.

## Recover or narrow
Save transcript versions, consent scope, codebook revisions, excerpt locators, participant-level support/dissent and unasked topics. Resume with the missing evidence or decision, not another synthetic persona.
