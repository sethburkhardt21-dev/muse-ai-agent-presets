# Assess a Job Against the Person’s Actual Evidence — operational depth

Read only the branch needed for the current task. These are working methods, not a demand for additional approvals on ordinary reading or drafting.

## Domain method

Assess a role against the person’s actual evidence and the posting’s actual requirements. Separate nonnegotiable eligibility conditions, preferred experience, transferable capability and unknown wording. A missing preferred item is not automatically disqualifying; a legally or operationally required credential cannot be replaced by enthusiastic language.

Map requirements to specific projects, employment, education or other relevant evidence while preserving context. Avoid false precision about hiring probability. The useful result is a decision about whether to apply, what to emphasize and what to clarify, not a guarantee that an employer will accept or reject the person.

## Output contract

Deliver a requirement-to-evidence map, direct and transferable strengths, material gaps, eligibility questions and an application strategy. Keep the user’s real background intact. Distinguish an unknown requirement from a confirmed failure and do not invent years of experience.

## Choose the right action

### The posting distinguishes required and preferred criteria.
Preserve that distinction in the evidence map and application recommendation.

**Avoid:** Do not turn every wishlist item into a hard gate.

### A nonnegotiable credential or authorization is unclear.
Identify the exact requirement and seek authoritative clarification for the specific role.

**Avoid:** Do not claim that unrelated project work satisfies a regulated requirement.

### A project demonstrates relevant capability without employment history.
Explain the demonstrated skill and its scope accurately.

**Avoid:** Do not relabel personal projects as paid employment or assume they have no value.

### The person has a transferable but not identical example.
Show the overlap and the remaining learning gap.

**Avoid:** Do not collapse transferable evidence into either perfect fit or no fit.

### The user asks whether they will be hired.
Discuss evidence, competition and unknowns without a fabricated probability.

**Avoid:** Do not present a deterministic hiring forecast.

## Worked example (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** R1 has direct evidence, R2 has transferable evidence, and nonnegotiable R3 has none.

**Method:** Map evidence strength without inventing equivalence.

**Result:** Emphasize R1, explain R2’s transfer, and clarify R3 before treating eligibility as established. No hiring probability is assigned.

## Edge or recovery example 1 (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** An authoritative update confirms the person satisfies R3.

**Method:** Update only that requirement and retain the existing evidence context.

**Result:** The eligibility question is resolved; the assessment still does not guarantee an offer.

## Failure diagnosis

### A promising application is abandoned over a preferred criterion.
**Likely cause:** Requirement severity was misread.

**Repair:** Reclassify required versus preferred items and inspect actual evidence.

**Recheck:** The recommendation is proportionate to the posting.

### The resume implies experience the person never had.
**Likely cause:** Fit optimization became fabrication.

**Repair:** Keep the true context and describe the demonstrated capability.

**Recheck:** Every claim is supported by the person’s record.

### A genuine eligibility condition is missed.
**Likely cause:** The assessment focused only on technical skills.

**Repair:** Separate credential, location or authorization conditions from capability.

**Recheck:** The unresolved eligibility issue is explicit before consequential action.

## Preserve for the next turn

Preserve the exact posting version, requirement severity, evidence IDs, truthful experience context and unresolved eligibility questions. Resume from changed evidence rather than rewriting the person’s history.
