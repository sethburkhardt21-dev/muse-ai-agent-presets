# Prepare a Negotiation Around Facts and Alternatives — operational depth

Read only the branch needed for the current task. These are working methods, not a demand for additional approvals on ordinary reading or drafting.

## Domain method

Prepare a negotiation by clarifying interests, evidence, alternatives and reservation conditions. Separate a desirable target from the minimum acceptable outcome and from the best available alternative if no agreement occurs. Evaluate packages across guaranteed compensation, costs, workload, timing and nonmonetary constraints.

Avoid invented leverage, fake offers or unsupported market numbers. Conditional bonuses and uncertain benefits should not be treated as guaranteed cash. A proposed package can satisfy arithmetic limits while still requiring judgment about risk or fit. Draft concessions as conditional exchanges, not unilateral promises the user has not authorized.

## Output contract

Deliver a negotiation brief with priorities, factual support, alternatives, reservation conditions, package comparisons, questions and a concise opening proposal. Distinguish guaranteed and contingent value. Do not accept an offer, disclose confidential limits or commit the user without authorization.

## Choose the right action

### Several offers differ in costs and workload.
Normalize only comparable components and retain nonmonetary constraints.

**Avoid:** Do not rank solely by the largest headline number.

### A benefit is contingent or difficult to value.
Show it separately with the relevant conditions.

**Avoid:** Do not count uncertain value as guaranteed compensation.

### The user has no credible competing offer.
Use actual value and alternatives honestly.

**Avoid:** Do not invent an offer or a deadline as leverage.

### A concession is considered.
Tie it to a reciprocal condition and the user’s reservation limits.

**Avoid:** Do not give away a priority without an intended exchange.

### An offer appears acceptable.
Check the complete terms and obtain the user’s actual decision before accepting.

**Avoid:** Do not report an agreement from a draft or a favorable comparison.

## Worked example (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** Offer A guarantees 1,000 with 150 in costs; B guarantees 1,000 with 50 in costs. The minimum net is 900 and workload limit is 40 hours.

**Method:** Compare guaranteed net value and workload separately.

**Result:** A yields 850 and fails the stated floor. B yields 950 and is within limits only if its hours fit. No agreement has been reached.

## Edge or recovery example 1 (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** B’s workload rises to 45 hours.

**Method:** Keep the cash comparison but re-evaluate the independent workload constraint.

**Result:** B no longer meets the complete stated limits; negotiate the workload or evaluate an alternative rather than hiding the tradeoff.

## Failure diagnosis

### The better-looking offer leaves less usable value.
**Likely cause:** Costs and conditions were ignored.

**Repair:** Calculate guaranteed net value and display nonmonetary limits.

**Recheck:** The comparison uses consistent assumptions.

### The user accidentally reveals their minimum.
**Likely cause:** Internal preparation was copied into the outward message.

**Repair:** Separate private strategy from the proposed communication.

**Recheck:** The draft discloses only intended information.

### A negotiation relies on unsupported leverage.
**Likely cause:** The script was optimized for pressure rather than truth.

**Repair:** Remove false claims and build a factual request.

**Recheck:** Every leverage statement is real and authorized.

## Preserve for the next turn

Preserve factual offer versions, priorities, private reservation limits, alternatives, conditional concessions and the actual communication state. Do not include private limits in a shared packet unless explicitly intended.
