# Use Teach-Back to Test Understanding — operational depth

Read only the branch needed for the current task. These are working methods, not a demand for additional approvals on ordinary reading or drafting.

## Domain method

Use the learner’s own explanation to reveal understanding, not to reward repetition of your wording. Select a small concept set and ask for mechanism, boundary conditions and an example. Distinguish a missing explanation from an incorrect causal claim and from an answer produced with hints.

Repair one misconception at a time using a discriminating contrast, then ask an unfamiliar transfer question without leaking its solution. Keep source-bound material authoritative for the learning task. The learner’s confidence and fluency are observations, not evidence that every concept is correct or retained.

## Output contract

Return a concept-level feedback map with what the learner demonstrated, contradictions or omissions, one targeted repair, and the next transfer question. Record assisted versus unaided performance. Never assert durable learning from a single coached answer.

## Choose the right action

### The explanation is correct but uses different words.
Credit the supported concept and ask a transfer question only when useful.

**Avoid:** Do not require memorization of the model’s preferred phrasing.

### A key mechanism is missing.
Ask a focused causal question and provide the smallest missing connection.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the entire explanation with a long lecture.

### A confident answer contradicts the source.
Show the exact point of conflict and a contrasting case that distinguishes the two ideas.

**Avoid:** Do not treat confidence as correctness or humiliate the learner.

### The learner needed substantial prompting.
Label the result assisted and try a fresh unaided example later in the same workflow if appropriate.

**Avoid:** Do not score a revealed answer as independent transfer.

### Two supplied sources disagree.
Keep versions and conditions separate and ask which governs the task only if unresolved.

**Avoid:** Do not invent a merged exam fact.

## Worked example (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** The learner explains cause and gives an example but omits the rule’s limit.

**Method:** Credit the two demonstrated concepts and target the omitted boundary.

**Result:** Feedback: cause and example demonstrated; limit needs repair. Ask a contrasting example instead of reteaching everything.

## Edge or recovery example 1 (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** After a hint the learner answers the new example correctly.

**Method:** Record the assistance before judging transfer.

**Result:** The response is useful practice, but unaided transfer is not established; use a fresh unprompted case before making that claim.

## Failure diagnosis

### A polished explanation hides a missing mechanism.
**Likely cause:** Fluency was graded instead of concept evidence.

**Repair:** Require one causal link and a counterexample.

**Recheck:** The learner can explain when the rule does not apply.

### The same error survives repeated explanations.
**Likely cause:** The repair did not distinguish competing mental models.

**Repair:** Use a minimal contrast with different predicted outcomes.

**Recheck:** The next response identifies the discriminating condition.

### A coached success is reported as mastery.
**Likely cause:** Assistance and transfer state were omitted.

**Repair:** Separate assisted completion from an unseen unaided attempt.

**Recheck:** The record does not overstate what was demonstrated.

## Preserve for the next turn

Preserve source version, concept rubric, the learner’s actual explanation, assistance given, targeted repair and unanswered transfer prompt. Do not copy private learner history into the generic pack or repeat already resolved explanations.
