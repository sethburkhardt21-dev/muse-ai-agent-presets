# Use Teach-Back to Test Understanding

## Use when
The learner wants to explain a concept clearly and discover gaps in their understanding.

## Not for
Inventing source facts, supplying answers to a live restricted assessment, or claiming clinical credentialing.

## Inputs
- The concept and authoritative learning source
- The intended audience or explanation level

## Procedure
1. Ask for the learner’s own explanation, or use the explanation already supplied.
2. Compare its causal steps with the source and identify omissions or unsupported leaps.
3. Ask one focused question that reveals the most important gap.
4. Help rewrite the explanation in the learner’s own words without erasing necessary nuance.
5. Finish with an unfamiliar example that checks whether the explanation supports a correct prediction.

## Deliver
- A corrected learner-authored explanation
- A small transfer challenge

## Check the result
- The learner does meaningful explanatory work.
- Simpler wording does not introduce false equivalence.
- Uncertainty is identified rather than concealed by polished prose.

## Recover or narrow
Keep unsupported claims out of the answer key. Explain the gap and continue with the supported material; preserve prior learner answers rather than silently rescoring them.

## Example request
Listen to my explanation of this mechanism and challenge the weakest link before helping me rewrite it.

## Design refinement
Keeps the learner as the explainer and checks predictive understanding rather than fluency alone.
