# Rehearse an Interview With Specific Feedback — operational depth

Read only the branch needed for the current task. These are working methods, not a demand for additional approvals on ordinary reading or drafting.

## Domain method

Rehearse the kind of answer the role actually requires, using truthful examples and a short explicit rubric. For behavioral answers distinguish situation, personal action, result and reflection; for technical answers inspect assumptions, method and checks. Feedback should identify a specific improvement rather than reward confidence alone.

Keep practice separate from assessment. An answer produced after hints is coached, and repeated exposure to the same question can inflate apparent readiness. Use a new prompt or changed constraint to test transfer, while preserving the person’s real facts and natural voice. Timing is a constraint, not a reason to omit the decisive action or result.

## Output contract

Deliver the interview prompt, observed answer, rubric-based feedback, a revised outline and one next practice target. Record coaching and time used. Do not invent achievements, claim knowledge of a particular interviewer’s private rubric or promise hiring success.

## Choose the right action

### The answer is clear but omits the person’s own action.
Ask for the specific decision or contribution they made.

**Avoid:** Do not let a team-level story stand in for personal responsibility.

### The example has an uncertain outcome.
Use the actual observation and state the uncertainty.

**Avoid:** Do not manufacture a metric or success to complete the narrative.

### The answer exceeds the available time.
Cut setup and repetition before removing the core reasoning or result.

**Avoid:** Do not optimize for brevity by making the story misleading.

### The learner needs hints.
Coach explicitly and label the result assisted.

**Avoid:** Do not treat a rehearsed answer as an unseen independent performance.

### A technical question has several reasonable solutions.
Evaluate assumptions and tradeoffs against the constraints.

**Avoid:** Do not reject a valid alternative merely because it differs from the model’s preferred solution.

## Worked example (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** An answer covers context and action but omits result, using 100 seconds of a 90-second budget.

**Method:** Evaluate coverage and duration separately.

**Result:** Keep the action, shorten setup, and add the actual result; the answer is ten seconds over budget and result coverage is missing.

## Edge or recovery example 1 (authored, synthetic)

**Input:** After coaching, the answer includes all rubric elements.

**Method:** Record that improvement as coached practice and try a fresh prompt if assessment is needed.

**Result:** The revised answer is useful rehearsal, not evidence of unaided readiness on an unseen question.

## Failure diagnosis

### Feedback is encouraging but not actionable.
**Likely cause:** The rubric is implicit or generic.

**Repair:** Identify one observable missing element and rehearse it.

**Recheck:** The next attempt has a clear change to test.

### The answer becomes polished but untrue.
**Likely cause:** The rewrite invented detail.

**Repair:** Return to the person’s actual evidence and simplify the claim.

**Recheck:** Facts remain traceable to real experience.

### Practice appears excellent but transfer fails.
**Likely cause:** The same prompt and hints were repeated.

**Repair:** Introduce an unfamiliar prompt under comparable conditions.

**Recheck:** The record distinguishes memorized delivery from adaptable understanding.

## Preserve for the next turn

Preserve prompts, actual answers, factual source examples, rubric, time budget, hints given and the next targeted practice condition. Resume from the specific weakness, not an invented ideal answer.
